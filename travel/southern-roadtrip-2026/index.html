Saturday, April 4 — Atlanta & Athens

Campus tours, civil rights history, and Southern comfort food.

7:00 AM

Land at ATL & Pick Up Rental Car

Arrive at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport. Pick up the rental car and head to breakfast.

Rental car for the week
8:00 AM

Atlanta Breakfast Club

Famous for chicken & waffles, shrimp & grits, and lemon ricotta pancakes. Arrive early — lines form fast, but service is quick once seated. Right next to the Civil Rights Museum.

249 Ivan Allen Jr Blvd NW Reserve on Yelp
9:30 AM

Georgia Tech — Campus Walk

Self-guided tour of Georgia Tech's Midtown campus. Hit Tech Tower, the main green, Bobby Dodd Stadium area, and the student center. Digital tour resources available at admission.gatech.edu.

~90 min walk Free
11:15 AM

National Center for Civil & Human Rights

Powerful museum next to the World of Coke and Georgia Aquarium. Interactive lunch-counter sit-in experience, Martin Luther King Jr.'s personal papers, and global human rights exhibits. Budget 90–120 minutes. Teens will find it deeply engaging.

100 Ivan Allen Jr Blvd NW $26 adults, $20 youth
1:00 PM

Quick Lunch — Centennial Park Area

Fast-casual options near the museum: sweetgreen, CAVA, Chipotle, and local spots around Centennial Olympic Park. Easy in-and-out to stay on schedule.

Walking distance
2:00 PM

Emory University — Campus Visit

Drive to Druid Hills for a walk around Emory's beautiful campus. Main quad, Woodruff Library, and the surrounding neighborhood. A top-tier research university with a different vibe from Georgia Tech.

~15 min from downtown
4:00 PM

Drive to Athens

Head east on I-85 to Athens — home of UGA. About 1.5 hours depending on traffic leaving Atlanta.

~1.5 hours
5:30 PM

University of Georgia — Campus Walk

Walk UGA's historic North Campus: The Arch, the Chapel, the main quad with centuries-old oaks. A classic SEC flagship with a gorgeous campus. Combine with a stroll through downtown Athens.

Free
6:30 PM

Dinner at South Kitchen + Bar

Reservation confirmed for 6:30 PM. Upscale Southern comfort — locally sourced, seasonal menu, craft cocktails. A great spot to end a packed first day.

Sunday, April 5 — Easter Sunday Road Day

Athens → Greenville → Seabrook Island. Note: Easter Sunday — some businesses may have limited hours.

8:30 AM

Depart Athens

Check out of Hotel Indigo and hit the road toward Greenville, SC. Grab hotel breakfast or a quick coffee before departure.

~2 hrs to Greenville
10:30 AM

Greenville — Falls Park & Main Street

Walk across the iconic Liberty Bridge over the Reedy River falls — one of America's best city parks (USA Today Top 10). Stroll down Main Street with its shops, galleries, and public art. Consider a quick Furman University drive-by if Wyatt is interested (10 min away).

Free 60-90 min stroll
12:00 PM

Lunch in Greenville

Easter Sunday — check hours in advance. Passerelle Bistro (French, overlooking the falls) or Jianna (modern Italian with park views) are great options. Many Main Street spots will be open for brunch/lunch.

Check Easter hours
1:30 PM

Drive to Seabrook Island

Head south on I-85 to I-26, then follow signs toward Charleston and the sea islands. Stop at a Harris Teeter or Costco (off I-26 near Charleston) to stock up the house for the week.

~3.5–4 hrs (with grocery stop)

Arrive at Seabrook Island

Settle into 1116 Ocean Forest Lane. Get your Seabrook Island Club amenity card from the office outside the front gate. Explore the beach, unpack, and relax after two days on the road.

1116 Ocean Forest Lane
Evening

Easter Dinner at Seabrook or Freshfields

Pelican's Nest (oceanfront at the Beach Club) or cook in with your groceries for a relaxed Easter evening. Freshfields Village (5 min drive) has additional options if open.

Monday, April 6 — Downtown Charleston

History, architecture, and world-class food in the Holy City.

9:00 AM

Drive to Downtown Charleston

Head into the historic district (~45 min from Seabrook). Stop at Angel Oak Tree on Johns Island along the way — a 400+ year old live oak, free to visit, and absolutely stunning.

~45 min to downtown Angel Oak: Free
10:30 AM

The Battery & Rainbow Row Walk

Walk the seawall along The Battery with views of Charleston Harbor, then stroll past the colorful Georgian rowhouses of Rainbow Row on East Bay Street. Continue up to the historic City Market.

~2 miles, 90 min Free
12:30 PM

Lunch at S.N.O.B.

Slightly North of Broad — a Charleston institution for Lowcountry cuisine. Shrimp & grits, pork chop, and seasonal specials. Located in a converted warehouse on East Bay.

Book ahead
2:00 PM

Old South Carriage Tour

One-hour horse-drawn carriage ride through the historic district. Covers 30+ points of interest with a knowledgeable guide. A great overview of the city's 350+ year history before exploring on your own.

~$48/person
3:30 PM

King Street Shopping & Exploration

Browse the shops, galleries, and cafés along King Street — Charleston's main commercial corridor. College of Charleston's campus is right here too if Wyatt wants a quick look.

Walking
6:00 PM

Dinner at Husk

Sean Brock's groundbreaking Southern restaurant at 76 Queen St. The menu changes daily based on what's in season — every ingredient sourced from the South. Book well in advance on Resy. Dinner 5–10 PM, $31–50 range.

76 Queen St Book on Resy

Tuesday, April 7 — Plantation & Fort Sumter

America's oldest gardens and the birthplace of the Civil War.

9:00 AM

Middleton Place Plantation

America's oldest landscaped gardens, founded 1741. 110 acres including gardens, house museum, stableyards, and Eliza's House. Take the guided garden tour (included) and the "Beyond the Fields" talk about enslaved people who built the plantation. Azaleas should be in bloom. Budget 3–4 hours. On Ashley River Road, halfway between Seabrook and downtown.

4300 Ashley River Rd ~$45/adult 9 AM – 5 PM
1:00 PM

Lunch at Middleton Place Restaurant

Dine on-site at the plantation restaurant — Lowcountry specialties in a setting overlooking the rice fields and Ashley River. Or grab something quicker in the café area.

On-site
2:30 PM

Fort Sumter Ferry & Tour

Take the ferry from Liberty Square (340 Concord St, downtown Charleston) to Fort Sumter — where the first shots of the Civil War were fired in 1861. The roundtrip is ~2.5 hours including time on the island. Book tickets at fortsumtertours.com in advance; ferries sell out.

340 Concord St Book at fortsumtertours.com
Evening

Dinner — Seabrook or Freshfields

Unwind closer to home base. Hege's at Freshfields Village (French bistro — duck confit, filet mignon) or Lost Isle on Johns Island (Southern, open-air, wood-fired seasonal dishes).

Freshfields: 5 min from Seabrook

Wednesday, April 8 — Seabrook Beach & Island Day

Slow down. Beach, nature trails, kayaking, and island life.

Morning

Beach & Nature Trails

Take the morning easy. 3.5 miles of pristine beach at Seabrook, plus nature trails right on the island — Hidden Oak Trail and Old Drake Trail are short, easy, and beautiful. Great for a morning walk before it heats up.

Free with club access
Midday

Kayaking or Paddleboarding

Rent from Bohicket Marina (just outside Seabrook gate). Paddle through the Lowcountry marshes and creeks — dolphins, egrets, and incredible scenery. Also great for a boat tour.

Bohicket Marina
Lunch

Pelican's Nest or Cap'n Sam's

Eat on-island. Pelican's Nest is open-air oceanfront at the Beach Club — seafood and cocktails with your toes practically in the sand. Cap'n Sam's Grill is poolside and casual.

Seabrook Club
Afternoon

Pool Time or Beach Club

Relax at the Beach Club pools. Or rent bikes and cruise the island's flat, shaded paths. Teens will love having the afternoon to decompress.

Club access
Evening

Cook In or Bohicket's Lounge

Take advantage of the house kitchen with groceries, or walk to Bohicket's Lounge for casual pub-style overlooking the golf courses. Palmetto Room for something more upscale.

Thursday, April 9 — Charleston Encore

Savannah day trip option, or more Charleston exploration.

Option A: Full Day

Savannah Day Trip

Drive ~2 hours to Savannah for its stunning historic district, Forsyth Park, SCAD campus, and River Street. Lunch at Mrs. Wilkes Dining Room (legendary family-style Southern — arrive early, it's cash only with long lines). Return by evening. Or stroll the 22 cobblestone squares with Spanish moss overhead.

~2 hrs each way
Option B: Half Day

Boone Hall Plantation + Sullivan's Island

If you'd rather stay local: Boone Hall Plantation in Mt. Pleasant (~$37/person, famous Avenue of Oaks). Then head to Sullivan's Island for a laid-back beach afternoon and fresh seafood at The Obstinate Daughter or Poe's Tavern.

Boone Hall ~$37/person
Dinner

FIG or The Ordinary

Two of Charleston's finest. FIG (Food Is Good) is intimate, seasonal fine dining on Meeting Street. The Ordinary is a stunning oyster bar in a former bank building — raw bar, Lowcountry seafood platters, and craft cocktails.

Book well ahead

Soak up the Lowcountry before heading north tomorrow.

Morning

Beach Morning & Seabrook Equestrian Center

Final morning on Seabrook's beach. If anyone wants to try horseback riding, the Seabrook Equestrian Center offers trail rides. Otherwise, a slow beach morning is the perfect way to wind down the Charleston leg.

Lunch

Freshfields Village

Your neighborhood hub between Seabrook and Kiawah. Coffee, shops, and a few last-meal options. Good spot for lunch and picking up any souvenirs before packing up.

5 min drive
Afternoon

Pack Up & Sunset Walk

Pack for tomorrow's early departure to DC. Take one last sunset walk on Seabrook's North Beach or through the marsh trails.

Dinner

Final Charleston Dinner

One last go. Any spots you missed — or a repeat of a favorite. Lost Isle on Johns Island is a great casual option close to Seabrook.

Afternoon

National Mall Walk

Walk south from the hotel (~20 min) to the National Mall. The Mall stretches 2.4 miles from the Capitol to the Lincoln Memorial. Stroll past the Washington Monument, WWII Memorial, Reflecting Pool, and Lincoln Memorial. Plan 2–3 hours for a comfortable loop.

~20 min walk from hotel Free
7:00 PM

Dinner at Old Ebbitt Grill

DC's oldest saloon (est. 1856) — steps from the White House. Famous raw bar with oysters from both coasts, classic American fare, mahogany interiors. Reservations on OpenTable or call (202) 347-4800.

675 15th St NW Book on OpenTable

Sunday, April 12 — D.C. Museums

Holocaust Museum, Smithsonian, and the Botanic Garden.

10:00 AM

United States Holocaust Memorial Museum

Powerful and essential. The permanent exhibition spans three floors and takes 2–3 hours. Free timed-entry tickets required — reserve at ushmm.org. Other exhibitions (Daniel's Story) don't need tickets. Open 10 AM–5:30 PM, last entry 4:30 PM.

100 Raoul Wallenberg Pl SW Free admission Timed tickets required
1:00 PM

Lunch at Jaleo

José Andrés' Spanish tapas in Penn Quarter. Jamón ibérico, patatas bravas, paella, and sangria. ~15 min walk from the museum. Open from 11:30 AM. Book on OpenTable or call (202) 628-7949.

480 7th St NW Book on OpenTable
2:30 PM

Smithsonian Museums

Return to the Mall. Top picks: Natural History (Hope Diamond, dinosaurs), Air & Space Museum (free timed passes required), or American History. All free, open 10 AM–5:30 PM. Budget 2–3 hours per museum.

Free admission
5:00 PM

U.S. Botanic Garden

Peaceful wind-down at the foot of Capitol Hill. Tropical, desert, and medicinal plant collections in the Conservatory. Outdoor National Garden has spring hours until 7 PM. Free, no tickets. Right at the east end of the Mall.

100 Maryland Ave SW Free
Evening

Dinner Near Dupont Circle

Head back toward the hotel and explore the Dupont Circle dining scene. Upscale bistros and casual neighborhood spots within walking distance of the Banneker.

Pro Tips

Book These Now

Husk (Resy), S.N.O.B., FIG, Old Ebbitt (OpenTable), Fort Sumter ferry (fortsumtertours.com), Holocaust Museum timed tickets (ushmm.org), Air & Space passes (si.edu).

Easter Sunday (April 5)

Many restaurants have limited hours. Plan for hotel breakfast in Athens and check Greenville restaurant hours before driving in. Stock up on groceries before arriving at Seabrook.

Seabrook Club Card

Pick up your amenity card from the office outside the front gate on arrival. It gives access to pools, Beach Club, restaurants, and recreation.

DC Metro Tips

Tap your credit card at Metro gates — no SmarTrip needed. Blue/Yellow lines from DCA connect to most stops. Trains every 12–15 min weekends.

Comfortable Shoes

Full days in Charleston and on the National Mall easily cover 5–8 miles on foot. Layers for mid-April — highs 65–75°F across the South and DC.

Angel Oak Tree

Free, on Johns Island between Seabrook and Charleston. 400+ year old live oak. Don't skip it — quick detour on any Charleston day.
